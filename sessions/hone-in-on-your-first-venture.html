Section 1

Understand the Investor Lens

Sunil explained how venture capital funds work and how those structures shape investor decisions.

  1. 1
    Study the fund

    Research the investor’s sector, stage, check size, portfolio, and preferred pitch style.

  2. 2
    Understand the timeline

    Consider how a fund’s 10-year structure shapes expectations for growth, follow-on funding, and potential exits.

  3. 3
    Show the potential

    Explain why the opportunity could become a large, meaningful company—not only a stable small business.

  4. 4
    Find the right fit

    If an investor does not invest in your sector, focus your time on someone whose focus matches what you are building.

Section 2

Build Evidence of Demand

Replace assumptions with evidence from customer conversations. Capture specific quotes, commitments, waitlists, trends, and other signals that show people want the product.

Make the story concrete

At the earliest stage, the strongest story is often simple: you found a meaningful pocket of demand, you understand it deeply, and you are positioned to build the solution.

  • Show what customers told you, not only what you believe they need.
  • Explain the customer impact before leading with technical features.
  • Use proxy signals such as search trends, customer commitments, or a waitlist when revenue is not yet available.
  • Show why you are credible to build the product.

Section 3

Build Your Pitch

Sunil recommended a clear, easy-to-understand structure for an early-stage pitch.

  1. 1
    State your company’s position

    Share the team, product, fundraising status, and traction.

  2. 2
    Define the unmet need

    Explain who has the problem and why it matters now.

  3. 3
    Show the customer pain

    Use direct conversations and specific evidence.

  4. 4
    Explain the solution

    Describe what you can build and why you are positioned to build it.

  5. 5
    Demonstrate demand

    Include customer quotes, commitments, waitlists, or other signals.

  6. 6
    Outline the next steps

    Share your roadmap and team.

Section 4

Put the Advice to Work

  • Rewrite your opening pitch in two or three simple sentences.
  • Collect specific customer quotes and measurable demand signals.
  • Clarify the customer impact your company could create.
  • Draft a short outreach email tailored to the investor’s focus.
  • Map the people you have spoken with and what you learned.
  • Focus first on finding highly motivated customers.

Section 5

Review the Founder Questions

Investor Fit

How should I decide which investors to contact?

Research each investor’s sector, stage, check size, portfolio, and preferred pitch style. If an investor says they do not invest in your sector, move on to someone whose focus matches your company.

How should I explain a shorter path to growth?

A shorter timeframe is not automatically a concern. Show that the opportunity can become a large, attractive company and that you can reach meaningful customer traction in a reasonable amount of time.

How should I pitch a hardware company?

Focus on customer demand and your credibility to build the product. Use shortcuts, prototypes, or other evidence where possible, and avoid overemphasizing patents or technical features without showing why customers want the product.

Customer Evidence

What evidence matters before I have significant revenue?

Customer conversations, specific quotes, waitlists, commitments, search trends, and other demand signals can help show that people want the product. Explain what you learned and how it shaped the solution.

Should I lead with the customer or the end user?

Start with the direct customer and explain the value they receive. Show the return on investment as clearly as possible, even if the early calculation is based on customer interviews and a working hypothesis.

Fundraising Strategy

How should I contact a venture capitalist?

Keep the email short. Use two or three sentences to explain what you are building, where you are in the process, and why the investor may be a fit.

What should I ask a venture capitalist after receiving an offer?

Once you have an offer, ask detailed questions about how the investor supports companies, including recruiting, customers, pricing, packaging, and follow-on funding.
